# Episode 03: Diagon Alley

The next morning, Hagrid led Harry out of the island cottage and onto the rain-dark streets of London. Harry could scarcely believe that the giant knew a world hidden inside the ordinary one. When they reached the Leaky Cauldron, Hagrid rapped on a plain brick wall behind the inn, and a door appeared.

Beyond it lay a crooked lane shining with spellbooks, cauldron shops, and crowds of witches and wizards. Hagrid bought Harry an ice cream and explained, patiently, that his parents had left him money in a vault at Gringotts. For the first time, Harry understood that he had not always been an unwanted child living in cupboards and on scraps.

Inside Gringotts, goblins counted coins behind long counters. Hagrid showed Harry the fortune his parents had left behind, then carried a secret package from Vault 713. Harry wanted to ask what it was, but Hagrid only winked.

“Best not mention it,” he said.

Their next stop was Madam Malkin's, where Harry bought his robes. Hagrid left him to try on a set of black cloth, and while Harry waited, a pale boy entered the shop. He spoke about Hogwarts with confidence and sneered at Hagrid, though Harry could not say why the boy's certainty made him uneasy.

At Flourish and Blotts, Harry saw shelves of thick spellbooks. At the apothecary, jars of herbs and strange ingredients crowded the windows. But the last shop in the alley drew Harry closer than the rest.

**OLLIVANDERS**

**MAKERS OF FINE WANDS SINCE 382 B.C.**

A gray-haired man appeared from between the shelves as if he had been waiting. He found no fewer than a dozen boxes that might contain a wand for Harry. One snapped a glass; another made Hagrid's hat burst into flames. Finally, Mr. Ollivander handed Harry a wand of holly and phoenix feather.

Warmth rushed from the wood to his fingers. A golden light filled the dusty shop, and Ollivander stood very still.

“Curious,” he murmured. “Very curious.”

Harry thought he saw a flicker of fear in the wandmaker's eyes.

“The phoenix whose feather is in your wand gave another feather just once,” Ollivander said. “The other wand gave you that scar.”

Outside, Hagrid noticed Harry staring at his new wand. When Harry told him what Ollivander had said, Hagrid shook his head.

“Don't think on it,” he replied. “Wands are funny. Some folk's pasts catch up faster than others.”

But as Hagrid led Harry from the alley, a dark-robed stranger slipped around the corner and vanished into the crowd. Beneath his hood, a pair of eyes watched the boy carrying the wizarding world in a paper bag.
